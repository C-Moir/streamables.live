# Saturday News Posts: AI x Hospitality current happenings

A short take each Saturday on something happening RIGHT NOW in AI + hospitality. These can't be
pre-written; that's what makes them work. The Monday pack automation surfaces candidates; this
file is the manual workflow plus the first month done for you.

## The 10-minute workflow

1. Scan (5 min): The Caterer, Restaurant Online (BigHospitality), QSR Media AU, Hospitality
   Magazine AU, Restaurant Dive (US), Hospitality Technology. Pick ONE story with a number in it.
2. Frame (4 min) with the template below. The formula: **their news + your 20 years + one
   sharp opinion**. You are not a news service; the take is the product.
3. Post (1 min). No image needed for news posts, or reuse a relevant card. Tag the company
   involved if the take is positive or neutral; don't tag when you're critical.

## The template

> [The news, one sentence, with the number.]
>
> [Why it matters to a real venue, two or three sentences. Translate from press-release to
> kitchen reality.]
>
> [Your position, one or two sentences. From the pass, not the conference stage.]
>
> [Optional question to open comments.]

## First four Saturdays (ready to adapt - verify each is still current before posting)

### Sat 25 Jul - the profitability stat
> A stat from the 2026 Restaurant Technology Study that should reframe every AI pitch: 42% of
> restaurants report profitability challenges, and the industry conversation has finally shifted
> from experimental tech to measurable utility.
>
> Translation for operators: the vendors have noticed you're broke. Every tool you're shown this
> year will come wrapped in an ROI claim. Most of those claims assume your data is clean and
> someone on your team owns the tool. Neither is usually true.
>
> Before any AI subscription: know your numbers first. You can't measure a tool's return against
> a baseline you never captured.
>
> What's the one number you'd most want live visibility on this week?

### Sat 1 Aug - voice AI at scale
> Voice AI is now taking orders in nearly 900 US restaurant sites through a single vendor
> partnership, and the analysts are calling 2026 the year the AI-driven restaurant becomes
> operational necessity rather than novelty.
>
> The part nobody says out loud: drive-thru voice AI works because the interaction is narrow,
> repetitive and scripted. Your venue's hardest problems (prep planning, waste, rostering,
> handovers) are none of those things. The flashy front-of-house wins make headlines; the
> back-of-house data work makes margins.
>
> Twenty years in kitchens says: automate the boring layer first. It pays for the shiny layer later.

### Sat 8 Aug - "invisible AI"
> Industry analysts are predicting this year's shift is toward "invisible AI": systems quietly
> managing loyalty, pricing and demand forecasting rather than robots making coffee.
>
> Good. The best kitchen technology has always been invisible: the prep list that's right, the
> order that arrives before you run out, the roster that matches the bookings. If your team
> notices the AI, it's probably in the way.
>
> The question to ask any vendor: what does my team STOP doing once this is running? If there's
> no answer, it's a toy.

### Sat 15 Aug - the unified stack
> The big theme out of this year's National Restaurant Show: everyone is hunting the "unified
> tech stack". Ordering, payments, kitchen management and CRM in one system instead of nine
> subscriptions that don't talk.
>
> From two decades of watching venues buy software: the unified stack is a direction, not a
> product you can buy today. Whoever promises you all of it, today, is lying about at least a
> third of it.
>
> The practical version: fewer tools, each owned by a named person, each exporting data you can
> take with you. Consolidation you control beats integration you rent.
