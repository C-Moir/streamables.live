The delivery document. 10-14 pages maximum. Write it plain: the client is an operator,
not a board. Every finding needs a number; anything without a number is an observation,
and observations go in the appendix or the bin.

## 2. Findings in detail (one page each)

For each finding:
- **What I saw** - the evidence, specifically. Dates, quantities, who does what.
- **What it costs** - show the working. [units] x [$ rate] x [frequency] = $X,XXX/yr.
  Conservative numbers only; under-claim, over-deliver.
- **The fix** - concrete: the process change, the named tool and its monthly price, or the
  build. Who owns it, how long it takes.
- **Payback** - when the fix cost is recovered.

## 3. The 90-day roadmap

Maximum 6 actions, sequenced. Weeks 1-2, weeks 3-6, weeks 7-12. Each row: action, owner
(named person at the venue), cost, expected return. Nothing on the roadmap that requires
me - if they can do it all without ever calling again, the report is honest.

## 4. What NOT to buy

> This section builds more trust than everything else combined. List the tools/categories
> they've been pitched (or will be) that don't fit this operation, and the one-line reason.

## 5. Start capturing this data now

The 2-3 numbers the venue should start recording immediately, with the 3-minute EOD form
to capture them (adapted from the grill-tracker pattern). Even if they do nothing else,
in 8 weeks they'll have visibility nobody in the operation has today.

## 6. Appendix

- Systems inventory (every tool and spreadsheet, monthly cost, owner, verdict: keep/replace/kill)
- Staff conversation themes (anonymised)
- Method notes
